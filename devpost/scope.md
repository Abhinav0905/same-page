# Same Page

Same Page compares two people's memory of the same agreement and exposes the details they heard differently.

## The Unique Kernel

The app does not summarize a conversation or decide who is right. It compares two independent accounts across five concrete parts of an agreement: who, what, when, money and conditions. Each mismatch becomes one neutral question the pair can settle together.

## Who It's For

Roommates and other pairs making small everyday agreements over text or conversation. Today they assume they understood each other, then discover days later that "Friday," "half" or "after the bill arrives" meant different things.

## The Core Loop

Each person writes what they believe was agreed. Same Page compares the two accounts, separates matches from mismatches, then creates a short confirmation message. They edit that message together and leave with one shared version.

## Inspiration & Identity

It should feel like comparing two handwritten notes on a kitchen table: warm, calm and non-judgmental. The visual payoff is two separate accounts folding into one shared agreement card.

## Why This Matters to the Learner

The learner wants a second entry whose value is obvious to a general audience within seconds, with one memorable interaction instead of a large feature list.

## What "Working" Looks Like

Load a roommate-rent example. One person remembers "$800 by Friday" while the other remembers "half after utilities arrive." Press Compare. The app immediately marks who and topic as aligned, highlights the amount, due date and condition as mismatches, then produces a neutral text they can send. That is the demo's "oh, that's cool" moment.

## The POC Boundary

- One browser screen with two account inputs.
- One polished sample that works without sign-up or an API key.
- Structured comparison across who, what, when, money and conditions.
- Match, mismatch and missing-detail states with evidence from both accounts.
- An editable shared confirmation card with copy and reset actions.
- Local-only processing and responsive design.

## Later

- Live model support for open-ended agreements beyond the demo patterns.
- Share links for the second person to answer on another device.
- Saved agreements, reminders and multilingual input.

## Explicitly Cut

- Accounts and a database: not needed to prove the comparison loop.
- Recording or transcribing conversations: adds privacy and permission issues.
- Deciding who is correct: the product surfaces mismatch, not truth.
- Legal contracts, mediation and relationship advice: too high-stakes for this proof of concept.
- Automatic sending: the user must review the shared wording first.
